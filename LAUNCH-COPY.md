# Launch copy — Life on Paper

Voice rules, because this is the whole product: plain, warm, specific, and never
salesy. No "unlock", no "revolutionary", no "a testament to", no "tapestry".
Say the true thing.

The product, in one line, for reference:

> Write a few lines a day. Get a book of your life.

---

## 1. The waitlist email

This is your warmest audience — they asked to be told. One email, no drip.

**Subject:** `Your book is ready to start`

**Preview text:** `The thing you signed up for is live.`

**Body:**

> Hi,
>
> You asked us to tell you when Life on Paper was ready. It is.
>
> **lifeonpaper.app**
>
> The idea is small. You write a few lines a day — messy, mixed-language,
> whatever comes. We quietly clean them without rewriting you, file each one
> with the people and the years it belongs to, and over time weave them into
> chapters. At the end of a year, it is a book you can hold.
>
> Three things worth knowing before you try it:
>
> - **Your words stay yours.** It tidies; it does not rewrite. Hinglish stays
>   Hinglish, Hindi stays Hindi. Nothing gets translated into someone else's
>   voice.
> - **No organising, no streaks, no guilt.** There is no tagging, no
>   notification nagging you to keep a streak alive.
> - **You can start without an account.** Your pages live on your device.
>   Signing in only adds a copy so your book follows you between your phone and
>   your computer.
>
> If you want to see what a finished book looks like first, there is one to
> read here: **lifeonpaper.app/sample** — written the way people actually
> write, with the original words still underneath.
>
> It is early, and I would genuinely like to know what you make with it. Reply
> to this email and it comes to me.
>
> — Krishna
>
> *You are getting this because you left your address at lifeonpaper.app. If
> you would rather not hear from us again, reply and say so — I will remove
> you.*

Notes: one ask, one link, one reply invitation. The unsubscribe line is plain
because there is no sending infrastructure yet — you are removing people by
hand.

## What not to say

- Do not call it an "AI journal". The AI is the least interesting part; the
  promise is a book.
- Do not promise a printed book yet unless it is actually shipping. Say "what we
  are building toward" (the waitlist line already does this honestly).
- Do not say "unlimited" — the AI has a real credit limit.
- Do not claim privacy you cannot prove. The true version is strong enough:
  your words stay yours, nothing is published, nothing is sold, and the editor
  is asked not to retain or train on them.
- Do not describe features as "seamless", "effortless", or "magical". Show what
  it does instead.

## First-week follow-up (send only if it is true)

Wait three or four days. Ask one question, not five:

> **Subject:** `What did you write first?`
>
> Hi — I am the person who built Life on Paper. Genuinely curious: what was the
> first thing you wrote?
>
> I am not looking for praise. The most useful thing you can tell me is where
> it felt wrong — a title it got wrong, a memory it filed in the wrong place,
> anything that made you stop.
>
> Just reply.
