# Product Brief — "A Book of Your Life"

**One-sentence pitch:** *Write for ninety seconds a day. Get a book of your life.*

**Product in one paragraph:** A journaling app that treats a user's journal not as a pile of dated entries, but as the story of a life — like a book. Every entry is quietly polished, automatically filed into the eras, people, and threads it belongs to, and woven over time into chapters. The app remembers characters and timelines across months and years, so a memory written today connects to one written next year. It asks nothing of the user — no organizing, no tagging, no setup — and, at the end of each year, presents the user with a real book of their life.

---

## 1. The core idea

A journal is not a log. It is a life story, and a life story — like a book — has:

- **Characters** — the people who appear again and again (individuals *and* groups: "amma," "Rahul," "college friends," "the cousins").
- **Timelines / eras** — the periods of a life (childhood, school, college, first job, the present).
- **Threads** — recurring themes that run through the writing.

Today's entry about a childhood memory and next month's entry about another childhood memory are *the same storyline*. The app detects this and attaches each memory to the right character and the right era, quietly, forever.

**The structure of the book** (the librarian's model):

> **The Book of You** → *Parts* = eras of life → *Chapters* = woven narratives of weeks → *Moments* = original entries, nested beneath.

A memory that belongs to multiple storylines (a school friend in a childhood era) physically lives in one part — by time — and is softly cross-linked to every person and place it touches, like a scene in a real book that advances several threads while sitting in one place.

---

## 2. The key principles (decided together)

1. **Invisible magic** — the app does its work quietly. The user never manages, never organizes, never "files" anything.
2. **User touch always** — the app improves, never replaces. The user's words, voice, and language are always preserved; the original raw text is never thrown away.
3. **No homework, no guilt** — no setup questions, no feature tours, no streaks, no scores, no shame. Ever.
4. **The user is the author; the app is the editor and the librarian.** Both occasionally hand the author something worth reading — but the author always holds the pen.
5. **Kind curation** — when the app knocks, it brings warmth. It never ambushes the user with wounds.
6. **Trust is the foundation** — the user hands over their entire inner life. Local-first, encrypted, never used to train models.

---

## 3. Writing — the daily moment

- **Every way in is open:** a blank page, a gentle guided prompt, or voice.
- **Languages:** English, Hindi, and Hinglish from day one. The book stays in the language the user wrote. Hinglish is a *choice, not a mistake* — the mix is part of the user's authentic voice and is never "fixed" into standard English or Hindi.
- **The writing surface:** a warm, paper-like page. A faint daily question breathes where the user would type (*"What made today different?"*) — no prompt UI, no buttons; the user just types over it. It changes daily.
- **Mic** sits quietly at the bottom for talk-to-write. A quiet "spark" offers a hand when stuck (a memory prompt, a person prompt, a voice day) — one tap, never more.
- **Continuous auto-save** — every word saved as it is written; there is never a save button.
- **Media (chosen: photos + voice keepsakes):** a moment may carry words, photos, and recorded sound. Two distinct kinds of voice: *talk-to-write* (sound is input; the text is the treasure) and *keepsake capture* (the audio itself is the treasure — a laugh, a song, a voice — saved forever exactly as recorded).
- **Media-only days are valid pages** (chosen): some days the moment *is* the photo or the clip. The app may add one soft, factual contextual line (*"The sky, one evening in October"*) — never writing for the user.
- **Finishing feels like closing a diary**, not submitting a form.
- **The feel:** five quiet minutes with a friend who listens — not an app session.

---

## 4. The invisible polish (the editor)

- Every entry is quietly polished: grammar, spelling, awkward sentences — with a **very light touch**.
- The goal is the same person, on their best day — not a novelist's voice replacing the user's.
- **"Fix, don't rewrite. Preserve the writer's voice. If in doubt, do less."**
- **Raw words are always kept beneath** — one tap deep, mostly never seen, but always there.
- **The one-time reveal** (decided): the polishing stays invisible — *except* once, when a transformation is genuinely dramatic (almost always a voice note), a single gentle "before → after" moment shows itself. Once, ever. Then silence.

---

## 5. Automatic organization (the librarian)

- After every entry, the app quietly figures out **who** is in it, **what era of life** it is about, and which threads it touches — and files it. Zero user action.
- **Characters are individuals and groups**, remembered in depth (see Reading → People).
- **Eras are detected, not declared.** The app recognizes "this belongs to that part of your life" — distinguishing *when you wrote it* from *what period it is about*.
- When a user writes about the same era again months later, the new memory attaches to the existing timeline — the story grows.
- The app distinguishes the **raw diary** (as written) from the **woven story** (polished, connected) — both always exist.

---

## 6. Woven chapters (the storyteller)

The app quietly weaves each period's short entries into a flowing narrative — the user never writes these; the app does, from the user's own words only.

**The voice contract (every chapter obeys):**
1. **The narrator is the user** — first person ("I"), their language, their register, their cadence.
2. **Chronological truth, written with literary craft** — the week in its true order, but smooth, rhythmic, and careful. Never rearranged for drama, never invented.
3. **Nothing invented, nothing diagnosed** — every sentence traces to something the user wrote; no events, feelings, or motives added.
4. **Describe, don't interpret; land softly, never moralize.** Chapters end at the week's true last moment — the user feels *seen*, not *taught*.
5. **Light touch.** When in doubt, do less.
6. **Honesty about silence** — *"the rest of the week was quiet"* rather than a fabricated bridge.
7. **The raw is always beneath** — the toggle is the permanent check on the weave.

**Titles (chosen):** only when earned — drawn from the user's *own* phrases where possible (*"The week of the new routine"*). When nothing fits, the chapter keeps only its dates.

**Chapter rhythm (chosen):** the *first* chapter arrives as a surprise, whenever there is real material to weave. After that, a gentle weekly rhythm.

**Thin weeks (chosen):** a chapter is woven only when the week has substance. Thin weeks stay raw and honest; when material pools, a later chapter covers the span (*"these past two weeks…"*). The book never fabricates fullness.

---

## 7. Reading & browsing (the library)

**Navigation — three calm doors, nothing else:**
- **Today** — the writing page.
- **Story** — opens to a **bookshelf**: the eras of life as volumes (*"School Years," "College," "The Present"*), each showing its years; the current volume sits slightly open. The shelf *is* the table of contents and grows on its own when life enters a new era.
- **People** — the cast.
- One **discreet search** in the corner — it feels like asking the librarian: understands people, places, eras, phrases, even feelings; works across languages; every result drops into the book at the exact moment, with context.

**Inside a volume:**
- A **flowing river** of woven chapters, raw moments nested beneath and expandable.
- A quiet **"Story / As written" toggle** — the book *and* the true diary, one gesture apart.
- A **page view** for sitting with one chapter: warmer paper-like tones, larger gentler type, nothing but the words — "the reading lamp."
- Search results and character links jump you to the exact spot.

**People — the cast gallery and portraits:**
- Every individual and group the app has noticed, presented naturally (never "managed").
- Tap a character → **a portrait assembled** (chosen), not a cover-to-cover read:
  - Who they are in the story (the name the user uses; eras; number of moments).
  - **Where they appear** — eras and threads, softly linked.
  - **How you wrote about them** — a gentle feeling-arc across time, always traceable to the user's words, never a verdict.
  - **Their moments** — a browsable collection, newest first, each jumping into the volume.
  - **Quiet milestones** — first mention, a long silence, a return.
- **Groups** also show how their membership shifted through the years, from the user's own words.

**The reading principle:** calm, book-like, never app-like. The user holds their life in their hands.

---

## 8. The memory tutor & intelligence

**The private memory graph:** a quiet, private model of *the user's* story — people, groups, places, eras, threads — with confidence on every link. It belongs to the user alone, is updated after every entry, and is never used to train anything.

- **Invisible self-healing** is the default: new evidence quietly corrects old links.
- **Identity (chosen):** confident links ("amma" = "mom") merge silently. The tutor speaks only at true forks where a wrong guess would corrupt a character page (e.g., two different "Rahul"s).
- **The tutor's question types** — identity, eras, places, groups — each asked only at genuine uncertainty, in one warm human sentence, answered **Yes / No / Not sure**. "Not sure" is always safe.
- **Placement (chosen):** the tutor appears only when it has real questions — at most weekly, a small batch of two or three — as a gentle in-app card when the user opens *Today*. Never a push. Silent weeks stay silent.
- **The reward is visible:** an answer visibly heals the book — two Rahuls become one page — which makes answering feel good, not dutiful.
- **Boundaries:** never about feelings, never organizing work, never a quiz, never repeats, never explains its own machinery.

---

## 9. Tone & kind curation

**The tone engine** reads each entry's emotional register — conservatively, treating uncertainty as neutral, and **never showing its labels to the user**. Feelings are read only to guide quiet things.

**The kind curator** — the rule: when the app knocks, it brings warmth (growth, learning, good moments); when the user opens the book themselves, everything is present honestly. The app chooses its invitations carefully; it never hides the truth.

**What's safe to surface is learned, not assumed (chosen):**
- Conservative by default: anything uncertain is treated as "browse only."
- When the app surfaces something and the user softly dismisses it ("not today" — one gentle tap), the app learns that *this kind of moment stays uninvited* from now on. Each user's book learns its own boundaries with no management.

**Layered care for hard times (chosen):**
- **Sustained heaviness:** if many entries across weeks are heavy, a *rare, warm, non-diagnosing* check-in: *"I've noticed you've been writing about heavy things lately. I'm here for all of it — and sometimes, when it gets too heavy to carry alone, talking to someone real can help."* With discreet access to real helplines.
- **Acute signals:** a gentle, immediate, non-clinical response with real, verified, region- and language-appropriate crisis resources shown plainly. Never blocking, never alarming, never overstating certainty — the app notices because it cares, not because it is watching.

## 10. Onboarding & the first week

**Day 1:**
1. **The animation** — a life forming out of ordinary lines: entries drop in, faint labels settle on them ("amma," "college"), a thread connects two distant entries, weeks condense into a flowing paragraph, chapters stack, the spine grows into *"Volume One."* Then it dissolves: *"And it starts with one line. Yours, today."*
2. **The writing page** — warm, empty, one faint question.
3. **First entry** — typed or spoken, in the user's own Hinglish. Invisible polish applies; a single "before → after" reveal shows itself only if the transformation is dramatic (usually voice). Once, ever.
4. **A whisper:** *"I noticed 'amma' — I'll remember her."*
5. **One language question** — so polish, chapters, and the book match the user's voice.

**Days 2–3:** the faint question changes daily; some days voice, some typed. The app stays silent and present.

**Days 4–5:** the first quiet echo — *"You've mentioned [school] a few times — a thread is forming."*

**The first chapter** arrives as a surprise when there is real material to weave — the single most important trust moment of week one.

**After that:** ceremony — the gentle weekly rhythm — plus silence, warmth, and the gap behavior below.

**Never done:** tutorials, feature tours, menus, life-stage questionnaires, setup homework.

---

## 11. Habit & rhythm

- **The daily goal:** short, 90-second entries. Friction is the enemy.
- **Reminders (chosen):** no daily pings, ever. The app is silent while the user writes regularly, and only sends a warm nudge after a few quiet days — *"Haven't heard from you in a bit. No pressure — just checking in."* (Never more than the occasional warm line; always one tap to quiet it forever.)
- **Returning after absence (chosen):** warm and guilt-free — *"It's been four days — want to just tell me about today?"* The book simply continues. No catch-up homework, no streaks.
- **Surfacing the past (chosen: all three, coexisting):**
  - **Browse** — the user freely explores the shelf whenever they wish.
  - **The past visits them** — *rarely and well-timed*: "this day last year" a couple of times a week, a monthly "your year so far," the occasional meaningful anniversary. Never daily bombardment — and always only warm, growth-filled material (never wounds uninvited).
  - **Insight** — occasional, gentle, grounded observations ("You've mentioned [friend] in six entries this month"), never psychology.

---

## 12. The year-end book

The destination that gives every daily entry meaning.

- **Digital keepsake first:** the year assembled into a beautiful, designed book — woven chapters in order, the year's characters and their quiet moments, the learnings and good memories. Browseable, downloadable, shareable.
- **Physical print later:** a real, bound volume — the 90-second daily habit literally becomes an object on a shelf.
- **Gift books someday:** smaller books for the people in the user's life (*"a book about your father"*).

The loop: write for 90 seconds a day → the editor polishes invisibly → the librarian files everything → weeks weave into chapters → the memory gets smarter every week → and one day: *"Your year is a book."*

---

## 13. Privacy & trust (the foundation)

**Chosen: local-first, cloud for the clever parts.**
- Words live on the user's device, first and always — writing, reading, searching are instant and offline. The journal is *theirs*.
- Only the heavy magic (weaving, deep memory, the tutor) goes to the cloud — encrypted in transit, **never used to train any model**, with a visible record of what was processed.
- A user who wants zero cloud still gets a working core journal (writing, polishing, basic grouping on-device).
- **The hard line:** user memories are never used to improve our models. This single promise is the whole trust foundation.

---

## 14. The app's voice

The app is a **caring presence, not a system.** Every message must pass one test: *"Would I believe a kind person wrote this?"* If it sounds like an AI notification, it is rewritten.

- Warm, interested, zero guilt, no demands.
- Notices when the user goes quiet — but never makes them feel managed.
- Speaks like a friend who knows you are busy and cares anyway.

---

## 15. What we deliberately never do

- No organizing, tagging, or filing by the user.
- No asking the user to name their eras or life stages — the book emerges.
- No feature tours, menus, or dashboard clutter.
- No streaks, scores, rewards, or guilt mechanics.
- No notification bombardment.
- No invented events, feelings, or diagnoses in any weave or summary.
- No ambushing the user with painful memories.
- No using user writing to train models.

---

## The complete loop, in one picture

> Write a few lines a day, any way you like → invisible polish, your voice kept → the librarian files every memory to its people and eras → weeks weave into chapters in your own voice → the memory learns your world, gently and privately → the past visits kindly → and the year becomes a book of your life.

**Write for ninety seconds a day. Get a book of your life.**
